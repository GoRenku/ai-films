Publishing as **javiexmachina**, Javier Fan presents a connected music-video practice rather than an isolated generation test. His project site credits **javier** for direction and editing and **slopdiva** for the song on **I’M YOUR SLOP (I’M YOUR ART)**. The original channel’s October 11 release runs **4:07**. Slopdiva is a credited project identity; the checked sources do not establish a separate human performer.

## Why follow this artist

The interesting decision is selection. His public process compares musical versions at specific timestamps, plans pictures against the song and reuses characters from **ANYDAY NOW** and **PERMANENT UNDERCLASS**. A domestic image—a woman washing dishes beside a television—is connected to a synthetic diva wearing a mask of her own face. That gives the work an authored relationship between ordinary labour, performance and artificial identity.

The site also shows practical interfaces for a shot sheet, a used/unused clip library, version comparison and frame-accurate edit decisions. These are concrete ways to organize judgment. They are not evidence that code independently supplied the final artistic choices.

## Disclosed tools, with version boundaries

The [public process page](https://javiexmachina.com/process/) names **Suno** for songs and **Claude** for cutting and mixing tests, then presenting versions on a web page for the creator to listen and decide. It names **Midjourney** for images and describes AI motion without identifying a specific video model. Exact tool versions and the complete final-release tool inventory remain unknown.

That page documents an earlier **3:23 development edit**, while the released video is 4:07. The release announcement and current project site also give different counts of generated and selected clips. Their scopes are not reconciled, so neither set is presented as an audited final production total. Development-page counters are not final-film credits or measured efficiency gains.
