**JeffTriesThings** publishes under that public account name. The complete [October 27 music video](/films/jeff-untitled-music-video/) is enough to establish an authored release; a full personal identity, official song title and separate musician remain uncredited.

The reason to follow this early work is the specificity of its production account. The creator identifies street, café and bedroom continuity as the problem, rather than treating a good isolated image as a completed scene. Krea 2, ChatGPT and some Qwen 2.1 are named for images, with H3 for video and lip sync. Those disclosures belong to this work; the wider portfolio is not represented as an established filmography.

## October 31 — repairing a face through camera movement

A [new demonstration](https://x.com/JeffTriesThings/status/2116226987601928468) and [full method reply](https://x.com/JeffTriesThings/status/2116227182855102780) describe a Python, OpenCV and ffmpeg repair: choose a clean face frame, track nearby features and camera motion, smooth that movement, then warp and feather the patch. ECC alignment refines its fit to the changing face.

The useful idea is to preserve a successful moving shot while repairing a local identity defect. This account belongs to the new demonstration; it is not a retroactive tool credit for the earlier untitled music video or an independently benchmarked fix.

## November 2 — extending a performance across joins

A [new Minimax h3 demonstration](https://x.com/JeffTriesThings/status/2117059777922740474) joins four clips around an existing song. The creator's [method](https://x.com/JeffTriesThings/status/2117059785908531558) preserves the preceding clip's last **39 frames** while generating the continuation, keeping the song's timing fixed across both the overlap and new frames. The practical recommendation is to place a join during a vocal pause, allowing a few frames for mouth synchronisation to settle.

The interesting control is to hold a successful stretch of motion and its audio timing steady while extending the performance. Smooth seams and near-perfect lip sync are the maker's assessment, not an independent benchmark. This is a production demonstration, not a newly credited complete song-video release or a retroactive account of the earlier music video's workflow.
