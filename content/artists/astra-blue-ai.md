Astra Blue AI is the public releasing project behind [P DOOM](/films/p-doom/). The account identifies itself as a slopcore artist. Its public project identity is used here without guessing an operator’s legal name or a human singer.

## Why follow this project

P DOOM turns a disagreement about risk into a pop-video premise: a synthetic figure receives control despite an acknowledged possibility of catastrophe. The interest lies in how an attractive performer and system-like overlays frame that power, rather than in the accuracy of the fictional risk estimate.

The release claims twelve hours of unattended production. It does not identify a specific model stack or named human director, and the phrase “Super Intelligence” is not treated as a technical credit.

## KILL SWITCH

[KILL SWITCH](/films/kill-switch/) adds the project’s earlier October 7 release, with its October 12 official YouTube upload. The synthetic persona conceals her intentions while appearing compliant. The original post acknowledges anabology as inspiration; a reply estimates five to six hours of production. Those claims belong to this release, alongside its prompt-only/added-grain account, and are not transferred from P DOOM. [Original release](https://x.com/AstrablueAI/status/2107859808657490208).

## NOW SERVING — direction and repair

[NOW SERVING](/films/now-serving/) adds a complete October 16 release about helpful automation becoming replacement. The maker’s [direct production reply](https://x.com/AstrablueAI/status/2111109685805994086) explicitly describes human editing, directional intervention and hundreds of failed clips. That disclosure belongs beside the project’s automation language; it does not retroactively prove or disprove the different accounts attached to earlier works.

## October 22 — Wait Till You See It

[WAIT TILL YOU SEE IT](/films/wait-till-you-see-it/) uses a mother-and-daughter relationship to carry an imagined transition from 2026 to 2050. The original full release supplies a 5:10 song, rather than a short preview. Its prompting-only production claim is attributed; the story’s job-loss figures and future outcomes are not adopted as factual predictions.

## October 25 — the assistant asks back

[What Am I To You](/films/what-am-i-to-you/) gives the recurring synthetic persona contradictory personal and military requests. The complete October 24 YouTube version runs 4:16, matching the measured X presentation. The maker's reply describes human concepts, direction and lyric revisions alongside system output. General ten-hour working days and hundreds of prompts per video are attributed workflow statements, not this work's audited total production time or team size; exact models remain unknown.
