## Why it matters

Chroma is the largest open AI-media competition by volume: the organiser counts more than 6,500 projects from over 5,500 participants in its first season. Its film categories are the genres of a conventional festival, so AI work is judged by form rather than by tool. Sponsor tool awards sit in a separate layer; winning one only shows that the entrant used that tool.

## Season 1

Judging ran in three rounds, with a panel per category, and ranked results are published in the official gallery. [The Twin Earth](/films/the-twin-earth/) by Jacopo Zanetti placed first in Film - Sci-Fi & Fantasy, independently confirming an award its maker had previously reported. Didi_ok's [Didi Li](/films/didi-li/) placed third in the same category. Other first places went to [AMRIT](/films/amrit/), [The Decisive Moment: Midnight Drop](/films/the-decisive-moment-midnight-drop/), [The Ice](/films/the-ice/), [Kill The Rabbit](/films/kill-the-rabbit/), [Garden of Kin](/films/garden-of-kin/), [Humans: Playtime](/films/humans-playtime/), [Railbound](/films/railbound/) and [Remember Me](/films/remember-me/). The organiser's pages no longer date Season 1, so its deadline and livestream come from No Film School's launch coverage. The competition began in 2023 as Project Odyssey.

## Season 2

Entries opened on October 19, 2026. The official site gives a January 7, 2027 deadline, while the ElevenLabs announcement also mentions January 1; entrants should confirm on the official site. Finalists go to a public vote and jury judging, with winners announced on a February 11 livestream and an in-person gala planned for April 2027.
