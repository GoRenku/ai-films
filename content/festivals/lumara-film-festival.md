## Why it matters

Lumara is one of the few platform-backed AI festivals to put its finalists on a real cinema screen, and it pays its winners in cash as well as credits. Its categories reward storytelling and genre rather than one vendor's toolchain: entrants could use any AI platform, and only the Serenissima Award is tied to Venice. Most of its nine judges are working AI filmmakers.

## The 2026 edition

The organisers say more than 700 films were entered. Six Grand Prize finalists and category nominees were announced on October 16, and the seven winners were revealed at the Roxy Cinema in New York on October 26. The terms name Venice.ai and MoonPay as organisers, with Venice.ai administering the festival and paying the prizes.

[Candy](/films/candy/) took the Grand Prize, [Mianmian](/films/mianmian/) the Jury Selection (described in the terms as the runner-up) and [Ato de — Later](/films/ato-de/) the Get Shorty award for films under two minutes. Ryan Lightbourn's [Black Thunder](/films/black-thunder/) won the Serenissima Award and Production Pi's [FIX](/films/fix/) the Mouse Award; FIX had earlier received a Higgsfield honourable mention. REVIVER and JUST SAY SOLANA have no standalone release from their makers, so their links open at the points where they play in MoonPay's official winners programme. Seventeen further films are celebrated on the official site.

No second edition has been announced.
