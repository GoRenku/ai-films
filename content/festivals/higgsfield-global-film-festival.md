## Why it matters

With $1 million in cash, this is the largest prize pool among the platform contests the journal has checked. It also tested a rule that matters for anyone studying how AI films are made: every generated image and shot had to be produced inside one project on the platform, so each winning film's project is published alongside it. That makes the winners a record of process, not only a list of titles.

## The 2026 edition

Entries ran from August 10 to September 16, 2026. Higgsfield's creative team screened entries into a shortlist of 126 films, announced on October 10. An independent jury then judged the shortlist while paid subscribers voted for the Audience Choice award, which closed on October 15. The organiser's contest page lists 5,000 final films submitted, 67,223 projects started and 55,628 participants from 176 countries. The 67,223 figure, quoted in the shortlist announcement, counts projects started, not films entered.

[Listen to Meh](/films/listen-to-meh/), released by Buenos Aires' Purga Studio, won first place, followed by AIST's [Fukushima](/films/fukushima/) and [The Field Agent](/films/the-field-agent/); [The Tortoise and the Hare](/films/the-tortoise-and-the-hare/) took the Audience Choice award. The organiser credits winners by platform username only, so each film page explains how the maker was identified. The honourable mentions link to their projects on Higgsfield.

The final jury is not settled in the organiser's own material: the winners page and the August announcement name different panels. The exact day the winners were published is taken from the contest timeline.
