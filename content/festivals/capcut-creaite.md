## Why it matters

CRE[AI]TE is CapCut's own festival, so the organiser is also the maker of the tools every entry had to use: AI had to play a central role in each work and CapCut Video Studio a primary one. That makes it a platform competition, and it is listed with that disclosed. What lifts it above a hashtag challenge is the structure: published rules and judging criteria, four categories with their own requirements, a named international jury drawn from studios and AI filmmaking schools, and a published list of every recognised work. The Film category asked for human-led storytelling of at least two minutes, and the jury's stated criteria put the maker's point of view and story ahead of the technology.

## Editions

The first edition ran from June 10 to August 11, 2026, free and open worldwide, with US$200,000 in cash prizes. Thirty works were recognised: ten cash awards and twenty non-cash Spotlight Selections. Marcelo Pinto Coelho's nearly wordless stop-motion-style short [CLOSET](/films/closet/) won the Grand Prix. Justinas Vosylius's [They Came When We Left](/films/they-came-when-we-left/), about a young alien studying the village humans abandoned, won the Film category. The Series award went to asapgeng's crime-game satire grund thuft uto, Creative to Blue Foxes' Anything You Want, and Commercial to Victor Banya's Wonder Toy Store. Kate Polet's felt-puppet dramedy ChildFree received the Student Vision Award.

The festival site says the winners were announced in September; CapCut published the winning works on its YouTube channel between October 10 and 13. The Spotlight Selections, which include Julius Cea's THE STRAY FAM and ALLisWELL Studio's A Damp Day, are selections rather than awards and are not listed as results here.
