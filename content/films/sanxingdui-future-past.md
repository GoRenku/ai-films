An archaeologist discovers that symbols on ancient bronze artifacts may help humanity confront a future crisis of algorithmic dependence.

## Why this film matters

A synthetic cast sustained over a feature creates a different test from a showcase shot: character identity and performance must remain readable through an entire story. Its planned ordinary cinema release also exposes the work to expectations beyond AI-focused festivals.

Bona’s **October 23, 2026** nationwide Chinese release is reported by Yicai and AWN China. That was a forthcoming announcement at the October 1 check, not evidence of audience reception or commercial success. The trailer link is provided for reference; playback was unavailable during verification.

## October 23 — Chinese cinema opening

[AFP’s October 23 report](https://www.afp.com/en/chinas-first-ai-film-hits-cinemas-ticket-sales-lag) confirms the film’s Chinese cinema opening today. Maoyan’s [current news index](https://i.maoyan.com/client) also carries its opening-day announcement. This supersedes the earlier forthcoming status; it does not establish availability outside China or a free full-film stream.

AFP reports Bona’s production account of nearly 100 people working for more than two years, at a cost above **10 million yuan**. These are producer-reported scale figures, not independently audited totals. The report’s advance sales and forecast are not presented here as final box-office earnings, and its quoted preview reaction is not this journal’s review.
