## Why this film matters

Vincent is a gifted photographer who still works with an old film camera, and he keeps losing AI film contests. When a new one is announced, the entry requirement is Soul. The release calls it a satire about AI film contests and the people who bet everything on them, and its tagline says the organisers wanted more of him than the piece he already put in.

The joke has a precise target. Soul is the name of Higgsfield's image model, the film is itself an entry in Higgsfield's Talent Contest, and its title names a $200,000 first prize. Shestak turns the conditions of his own entry into the story: the contest circuit that now shapes so much AI filmmaking, the pressure to use the sponsor's tools, and the old argument about whether a generated image can carry anything of its maker.

## How it was made

Shestak wrote and directed the seven-minute short. The release credits Higgsfield as the platform, Seedance 2.5 for video and Soul for images. Crew size and schedule have not been disclosed. His earlier [The First Honest Day](/films/the-first-honest-day/) also built its drama from a premise about faces and identity.
