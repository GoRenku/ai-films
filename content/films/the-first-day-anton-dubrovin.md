## Why this film matters

Anton Dubrovin made The First Day for his great-grandfather, Senior Lieutenant Polikarp Sokolov, chief of staff of a rifle battalion stationed outside Grodno when Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941. The film moves in a straight line from a quiet family morning and a letter from the border to the alarm, the evacuation, the fighting and a last look at the wrecked camp. Its stated aim is small and specific: to give a name that survives only in an archive a face and a day.

That makes it a test of something generated film is often used for and rarely does with care: reconstructing a real historical place and person from fragments. The director says he mixed generated scenes with restored archival photographs rather than replacing them. It won first place at AI FilmFest Serbia 2026, chosen from more than 1,700 entries.

## How it was made

Dubrovin made it alone. His release notes name Seedance 2.0 and Kling 3.0 for video, Nano Banana 2.0 and Seedream 4.5 for images and ElevenLabs for sound, and list what generation was used for: building the scenes and period settings, keeping characters consistent, extending shots and restoring archival images. Editing, sound design and subtitles were done by hand. Since April he has posted further war shorts and an AI series on the same channel.
