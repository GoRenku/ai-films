## Why this film matters

The obstacle course gives camera movement a job: reveal the threats that a small creature must cross while maintaining a readable relationship between character and arena. Its disclosed workflow separates spatial planning from surface design. A rough 3D reference can carry a route and timing while the image references establish the finished world.

## Practical techniques disclosed

The [October 2 guide](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1MXARcTpDhzahT_r2LV974MfeOGaJFEZsaZujMqpTQSU/edit) describes building a stadium greybox from a locked location image, placing obstacles in order, animating a simple stand-in and revising near misses. It keeps cameras on one side of the action, then exports a fast 24-fps reference.

The [published scene prompt](https://x.com/PJaccetturo/status/2105285290436812964) distinguishes what to inherit from previs—route and timing—from what to reject, including flat shading and weightless movement. It separately specifies character motivation, gaze, water motion and diegetic sound without generated music. These are maker instructions being described as evidence, not guarantees of the model's behavior.
