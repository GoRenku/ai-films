## Why this film matters

Princess Mora escapes palace life by stowing away on a starcruiser flown by Darin O'Joy, a pilot brave in action and cowardly in feeling, with a bounty hunter close behind. Hooroo Jackson calls it a futurist melodrama: a space-opera romantic comedy presented in the old 1.33:1 frame.

It is the fifth feature in his catalogue here and pushes the variable-film idea he has been working through since [A Very Long Carriage Ride](/films/a-very-long-carriage-ride/) came out in two animation styles, [My Boyfriend Is a Superhero!?](/films/my-boyfriend-is-a-superhero/) let viewers choose the protagonist and [Strings](/films/strings/) had a randomised ending. Lucky Stars has ten endings, picked at random by a companion page. The question worth watching for is whether a romance can earn ten different resolutions, or whether the ending becomes a lottery.

## How it was made

Jackson made it alone on a stated budget of $1,000, with every image, voice, performance and piece of music generated. He keeps a recurring set of synthetic actors from film to film: Rogers and Joan return here in their fifth features with him, and Mora's voice is blended from five machine voices. The models and tools are not named in the release.
