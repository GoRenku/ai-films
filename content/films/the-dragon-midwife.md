## Why this film matters

For thirty-six years Elsbeth Amrein has delivered every child in a high Alpine valley. One autumn night she is led to a cave above the village where a mother dragon cannot lay her egg. The egg comes, the lord's hunters chase the mother over the pass, and Elsbeth is left with a hatchling she must keep secret until the thaw.

The film starts from a European folk legend, the midwife called out at night to a birth among the hidden folk. In most versions she is paid and sent home; here she is handed the egg. That turns a short tale into a winter of obligation. Its five chapters are named for the season and the hatchling's size, from small as a cat to wider than the cave mouth, so the danger grows in plain sight.

## How it was made

Thousand Words FilmS wrote the story and screenplay and discloses AI-generated visuals and voices. As with [Journey to the Center of the Earth](/films/journey-to-the-center-of-the-earth/), the release separates sources from invention. It cites the Lucerne dragon stone of 1421 and the Swiss dragon reports gathered by Kircher and Scheuchzer, and names Elsbeth, the dragons and the whole village as inventions.

The director's note explains two decisions. The hunt leader, Kaspar, was written as a child Elsbeth herself delivered, so the last scene turns on her hands and not on a weapon. And a scripted scene of her following a trail of glowing coals up the mountain was cut, so the film goes straight from a calf born in a barn to the dragon's scales.
