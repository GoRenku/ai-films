## Why this film matters

Every third night Lord Alain walks into the oak woods and leaves his clothes in a hollow stone, because on those nights he is a wolf and needs them to change back. Only his wife, Odile, knows. One evening the clothes are gone. Months later the King's hounds corner a grey wolf in the fog, and instead of fighting it bows to the King. In the royal kennels it becomes Ash, cared for by Gwen, a girl who sleeps among the hounds. Ash will not eat raw meat and takes half of her bread each night. When Sir Hugh and Lady Odile ride into court, the King promises that one more growl will see the wolf sent away.

The source is Bisclavret, one of the oldest werewolf tales written down in Europe, a Breton lay by Marie de France in which the wolf is never the monster. The studio's director's note says it wanted to tell the story through someone the original never mentions. Gwen is that person, a witness at the bottom of the court who notices the small habits that give the wolf away. The note asks viewers to remember the broken loaf, half for each of them, more than the transformation.

## How it is built

Like [The Red Wolf](/films/the-red-wolf/) and [The Clay Legion](/films/the-clay-legion/), the 30-minute film is cut in titled chapters: a teaser, a prologue in Brittany, five parts from The Kennel to Half for You, and credits. The description separates the inherited legend from what is new. The medieval setting and the clothes-in-a-stone premise come from the lay, while the kennel, Gwen, Ash and the bread are invented for this film.

Thousand Words FilmS credits its own story and screenplay, uses music from the YouTube Audio Library and discloses AI-generated visuals and voices. The models, crew and cost have not been disclosed.
