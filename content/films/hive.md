## Why this collection matters

Babel’s housing rule makes a social system into a source of immediate jeopardy: a new resident needs an existing person’s place. **VACANCY** follows Mercy, who profits from that rule before becoming a target. **KNOCK** shifts to Hollis’s point of view and a last night with his daughter Roach. The two films test the same world through different positions within its economy, rather than merely repeating its cyberpunk surfaces.

## November 21 — HUSH

**HUSH** brings Mercy back. After forty-one sleepless nights, a mother named Ana takes her daughter to the Quiet Works, a factory that sells calm in sage-green bottles. Mercy arrives the same night to audit it and finds that the factory's shortfall has already been assigned to Ana. The creator's description presents it as a story about comfort, complicity and the cost of silence.

The third film widens Babel's economy beyond housing: here sleep and quiet are paid for with somebody else's suffering. Returning to Mercy, now as an auditor rather than a seller of vacancies, lets the series show the same character inside a second branch of the system. The release again credits Daniił Vołkaŭ and discloses AI-generated visuals and voices; the models used have not been disclosed.
