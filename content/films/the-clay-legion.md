## Why this series matters

A thousand years ago an army chose to become fired clay to keep a door shut beneath a red hill. In the first episode the river below the village of Stonemill sinks and gives one of those soldiers back. Militia captain Tova wants to know what the village has pulled out of the water. The old potter Hadric knows more about the knocking under the hill than he says.

The studio's [director's note](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lD-m_MHcokA) gives the idea its turn: the army is a promise, not a weapon. What the villagers find is a warning rather than the danger itself. That suits a studio whose earlier fables, from [The River Mother](/films/the-river-mother/) to [The Samruk Keeper](/films/the-samruk-keeper/), hinge on how people treat something they do not yet understand. The note singles out Tova's refusal to break a soldier she cannot read.

## How it is built

The 22-minute episode is organised in titled chapters like a television episode: a prologue in the distant past, then four parts that move from omens to the soldier waking to the opening of the door. Thousand Words FilmS credits itself with the original story and screenplay, takes its music from the YouTube Audio Library and discloses AI-generated visuals and voices. The models, crew and cost have not been disclosed.
