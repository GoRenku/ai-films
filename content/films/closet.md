## Why this film matters

CLOSET is built on a mirror. M. gets a new wardrobe, hears something moving inside it, and decides to stay in the room anyway; on the other side of the wall, in the world the wardrobe has opened, a small monster called Mo. is going through the same night from the opposite direction. The two never meet. The film runs about seven minutes almost without dialogue, so the parallel has to be carried by staging and cutting between the two rooms, and the ending widens it with a third, much larger neighbour.

It won the Grand Prix, the top prize across all four categories, at CapCut's first CRE[AI]TE festival in 2026.

## How it was made

The look is what holds the parallel together: the boy and the monster are both made to read as handmade puppets, with resin skin, glass eyes, real fabric and practical set lighting. According to CapCut, Coelho fixed those qualities in a single base prompt in CapCut Video Studio and generated the scenes with Seedance 2.0 and 2.5. The release poster says the film was made in partnership with CapCut, which also ran the festival that awarded it. Coelho, who signs his work as Cello, posted the complete film on his own channel on August 11, the last day of the festival's entry period.
