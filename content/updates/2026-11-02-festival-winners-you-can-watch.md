## A circuit worth following

AI film now has a festival circuit of its own, and it moves quickly. The new [Festivals](/festivals/) section follows fourteen festivals and competitions that meet one bar: a named organiser, public rules, a jury or defined judging process, and results the organiser publishes. Each page sets out what is open for entries, what is coming up and who won. Deadlines and phases are worked out from the organiser's own dates, so a closed call never reads as open. Three calls are open now: WAIFF, Chroma Awards Season 2 and Torino Underground Cinefest's AI Visions.

## The winners, playable

Results are only useful if the films can be seen. Every winner was traced to its official release, from the creator's or organiser's channel wherever possible, and more than sixty winning films joined the collection. [Costa Verde](/films/costa-verde/), Léo Cannone's Best WAIFF Film, and [A Face Only a Mother Could Love](/films/a-face-only-a-mother-could-love/), which won at Runway, Reply and OMNI, are now playable in full from their makers or original publishers. Le Drip, second at Reply and a winner at OMNI, is on its director's channel.

Platform contests produced some of the strongest new entries. Purga Studio's [Listen to Meh](/films/listen-to-meh/) took Higgsfield's first prize, and Ryan Lightbourn's retro adventure [Black Thunder](/films/black-thunder/) won Lumara's Serenissima Award. Production Pi's paper-world [FIX](/films/fix/) won Lumara's young-audience prize after a Higgsfield honourable mention. [Kagome](/films/kagome/) won Curious Refuge's three-minute horror competition.

## Documentary and essay forms

Several winners use AI for non-fiction rather than invented worlds. [Railbound](/films/railbound/), directed by Jonathan Perry with [Le Drip](/films/le-drip/)'s Alex Naghavi as visual director, won Chroma's documentary category. Gavin Carlton's [The Sultana Disaster of 1865](/films/the-sultana-disaster-of-1865/) won Austin's documentary award, and Vadim Lasca's mockumentary [What Remains](/films/what-remains/) was a runner-up at AI Film Festival Japan. Ibraheem Diab's [The Beginning](/films/the-beginning/) won WAIFF's Best AI Emotion Film.

## What stays unplayed

Some winners have no public release the journal could find, and their pages say so instead of linking a re-upload. Where only an organiser's programme holds a film, as with two of Lumara's winners, the link opens at that point. Trailers, adverts and visual studies that won awards are linked from their results but are not catalogue films. The journal has not watched every winning film in full.
