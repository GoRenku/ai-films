## A Grand Prix and several kinds of short

London’s first OPUS Film Awards presented its prizes on November 14. **ICO’s [PRAY TO GACHA](/films/pray-to-gacha/)** won the Grand Prix, bringing a capsule-toy collector’s loneliness into a fantasy developed with Unplug. **Dj Marcelll’s [The Walkers](/films/the-walkers/)** won Best AI Short Film. Its undead family belongs to a wider comic project; the exact winning cut remains to be matched to an original stream.

Two winners already had catalogue records: **Jiaze Li’s [Candy](/films/candy/)** and **Edmond Yang’s [When You Run From Your Mom](/films/when-you-run-from-your-mom/)**. Their new awards are Best AI Feature Film and Best AI Documentary respectively. Those are the festival’s categories; Candy’s roughly nine-minute runtime and Yang’s animated form remain unchanged. Rechecking [Lumara’s official results](https://lumarafilmfestival.com/) also resolved a stale artist note: Candy won its Grand Prize, as the existing film and festival records already showed.

## A retirement-home breakout you can watch

**[The Siesta Job](/films/the-siesta-job/)** has a working full 2:45 release through CLOWD’s Rad screening room. Tamara Llorens credits **Miguel Picazo** as co-creator and discloses Dreamina partner-programme support, Seedance 2 and CapCut editing. Their elderly couple’s escape gives animated comedy a pair of protagonists determined to misbehave.

## Anime, a music video and two intimate worlds

**Ce Jiang / Lost Joker’s [Oni-Slaying Maiden](/films/oni-slaying-maiden/)** received Best AI Anime Film for the listed 5:32 work. **Selina Schneider’s [Checkmate](/films/checkmate-selina-schneider/)** received Best AI Music Video, using chess as its sustained visual idea.

**Caroline Kiessling’s [Department of Good Memories](/films/department-of-good-memories/)** received The Luminary Award, presented by She Builds with AI. Its archivist and glowing memory phials give emotional ambiguity a material form. **Luke Mazey / Slophouse’s [Kakehashi (The Bridge)](/films/kakehashi-the-bridge/)** won the People’s Choice Award for a Tokyo time-loop story about stopping to notice another person.

These six newly catalogued works have completed-festival evidence. Their public full-film playback remains unverified; that does not mean no video exists. Candy and Yang’s film retain their previously verified release links. The Siesta Job adds the newly checked full stream.

## Cannes’ current entry route

WAIFF’s official Cannes 2027 page replaces its older, closed FilmFreeway call as the submission link. The €30 stage ends January 31; the final deadline remains February 15.
