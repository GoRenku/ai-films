## CapCut's first festival

The [CapCut CRE[AI]TE AI Festival](/festivals/capcut-creaite/) joins the festivals section. It is a platform competition, and listed as one: entries had to use CapCut Video Studio as a primary tool, and CapCut's team sat on the jury alongside Caleb Ward of Curious Refuge, Justin Hackney and Kristyna Archer of Wonder Studios, Egor Belov and Sebastian Bondo Petit of WHY.CGI and Ryoichi Wada of TOKYO EPIC. What qualifies it is the rest of the structure: free worldwide entry, published rules and criteria that put the maker's point of view ahead of the tools, four categories, US$200,000 in prizes and a full list of the thirty recognised works.

The Grand Prix went to Marcelo Pinto Coelho's **[CLOSET](/films/closet/)**, a seven-minute, nearly wordless stop-motion-style short. A boy gets a new wardrobe and hears something inside it; on the other side, a small monster named Mo. lives the same night in mirror image, and the two never meet. CapCut says Coelho locked the handmade look (resin skin, glass eyes, real fabric) in a single base prompt reused across every scene, and the poster credits the film as made in partnership with CapCut. Justinas Vosylius's **[They Came When We Left](/films/they-came-when-we-left/)**, already in the catalogue after its London win, took the Film award. The Series, Creative, Commercial, Student Vision and four honoree awards are listed on the festival page with links to the works.

## A festival in Belgrade

[AI FilmFest Serbia](/festivals/ai-filmfest-serbia/) is run by the Faculty of Contemporary Arts in Belgrade, with a six-member jury led by Ivan Đorđević, prizes paired with study scholarships and a published list of results that runs from the winners down to 209 official selections. It reports more than 1,700 entries from 126 countries.

First place went to Anton Dubrovin's **[The First Day](/films/the-first-day-anton-dubrovin/)**, a five-minute film for his great-grandfather, a battalion chief of staff at Grodno when Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941. It runs in a straight line from a family morning to the wrecked camp, and Dubrovin, working alone, mixes generated scenes with restored archival photographs rather than replacing them. He names Seedance 2.0, Kling 3.0, Nano Banana 2.0, Seedream 4.5 and ElevenLabs. Second place went to César Augusto Cabrera Boggio's AI Control, which the jury praised for its documentary approach.

## Long work, now in full

Daniił Vołkaŭ's **[Wrong Planet](/films/the-wrong-planet/)** was until now known here only from an excerpt and a paid audio listing. On November 7 he put the complete 71-minute film on YouTube for free: a crew's reports from planets that turned procedure into religion, among them a wellness colony that scores your calm and a meeting that has lasted 6,000 years. He made it alone.

Kavan the Kid's YouTube channel now carries the whole of **[The Chronicles of Bone](/films/chronicles-of-bone/)**: six prologues, each introducing one figure of the shared world (the Collector, Robin Hood, Captain Hook, King Arthur), and seven chapters of season one, and the series page lists them all, starting from Prologue One. Chapter Seven, No More Kings, released November 5 at about 28 minutes, strands Hook on Dead Man's Bay and sends him to the Mad Pirate King's cove.

## Two songs get their names

JeffTriesThings' two music videos were catalogued as untitled, because the X posts that carried them gave no titles. His YouTube channel resolves both. The October video is **[Lately](/films/jeff-untitled-music-video/)** and the November one **[Is That Too Much to Ask?](/films/untitled-music-video-jeff-tries-things-november/)**, both credited to Sadie, the recurring performer persona of his channel. He says he writes and produces the songs himself and builds each shot with video tools he has been tuning.

## Moving to YouTube

This week's playback pass moved two dozen works from X posts and platform pages to the makers' own YouTube releases. Among them, Magnific's **[SMOKE](/films/smoke/)** and **[Roots](/films/roots/)** now play from Magnific's channel, SMOKE's upload carrying its full credits, and Odair Faléco's essay film **[The Tool](/films/the-tool/)** plays from his studio zero1cine, with credits naming him for image, video, edit and sound.
