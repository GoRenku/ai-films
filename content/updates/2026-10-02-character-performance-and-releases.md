**SPARE** joins the collection as a dialogue-led short with disclosed Kling beta access. **Morgue Cat — Episode 1** turns the earlier production announcement into a public release.

## A documentary production experiment

Alex Patrascu’s **The City Beneath Your Feet** is presented as an approximately eight-minute documentary explainer. Its title is visible in his illustrated production brief. His [release thread](https://x.com/maxescu/status/2105619319220511010) attributes research, writing, generation, editing and delivery to Claude Opus 5.5 working through Higgsfield, with an **8h 21m** overall timeline and human intervention to choose the host voice. These are maker-reported results, not an independently audited measure of autonomy or factual accuracy.

His practical disclosure is more useful than the one-prompt framing: he [reports](https://x.com/maxescu/status/2105619328703836188) creating a **Seed Audio 1.0** voice sample and attaching it to speaking **Seedance 2.5** shots. He also [describes](https://x.com/maxescu/status/2105619336492990770) reusing one face image and voice sample across five locations, selecting 77 clips from 96 video generations. Those are scoped production claims, not a complete cost accounting.

The brief specifies a camera, lens and film-stock look. These are generation style instructions, not evidence that physical ARRICAM or Cooke equipment was used. A documentary’s synthetic visual realism does not establish that its locations or factual claims are accurate.

This explainer is included as craft coverage rather than a narrative-film collection entry. Its full factual argument has not been independently checked, and no full critical viewing is claimed.

## Two other approaches to performance

Simon Mayr’s [sponsored music video](https://x.com/simonmeyer_/status/2104635206439198880) discloses a specific lip-sync method: combine character stills with the actual music track rendered against a black frame. He thanks Ben Chimoy for help. The release is music-video and model-launch work, so it is covered as craft rather than selected as a narrative film. Camera-motion and reference-control claims remain his assessment.

Gavin Purcell’s [Present Company](https://x.com/gavinpurcell/status/2103640568576585932) discusses synthetic beings and unreliable narrators. The creator describes agent execution under his guidance and [estimates $108 in Runway credits](https://x.com/gavinpurcell/status/2103648447660429803), plus a Claude subscription. This is usage cost, not a full budget. It is covered here as a synthetic-media essay experiment; no narrative-film review is claimed.

Indra’s [painterly animation](https://x.com/IndraVahan/status/2104280029442331015) reports repeated frame selection with Opus 5.5. It is not selected for the narrative collection: the original release foregrounds a rendering experiment and provides no titled story or dramatic premise. That is an editorial decision about the released material, not a claim that the maker cannot tell stories.
