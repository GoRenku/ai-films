Three new music-video entries put recurring performers in different kinds of world. **[Don’t Let Me Scroll](/films/dont-let-me-scroll/)** builds a phone around Claudia; **[DOWNFORCE](/films/downforce/)** inserts her into a hybrid car edit; **[Coming Home on Fire](/films/coming-home-on-fire/)** continues Volta’s confrontational identity. Their credits remain distinct even where the same creator or character returns.

## Production records that show revision

anabology’s two making-of records distinguish planning, generated material, rejected takes and the director’s notes. Their dollar figures are scoped list-price generation accounts, not whole-film budgets. Software agent counts are not human crew sizes. The important public evidence is that particular failures were identified and corrected, rather than hidden behind an automation claim.

A separate Claudia software update announces Earshot audio analysis and optional ElevenLabs production audio. That is a capability disclosure, not evidence that either new film used those tools. The links on the artist page preserve that boundary.

## Narrative consequences at two scales

**[Wrong House](/films/wrong-house/)** turns an intruder into someone who needs escape. **[Dominio](/films/dominio/)** gives an imperial tournament a coercive political purpose. Vołkaŭ’s new short and Jun Pabon’s older, newly discovered release both give spectacle a dramatic constraint. The latter adds a new filmmaker to the collection, without promoting a festival-submission hashtag into an award.

## A precise note on language and acting

In his [new short chase’s prompt disclosure](https://x.com/Skyebrows/status/2111481963370402002), **Skye Freeman** says Japanese prompting is important to the style he sought. The published direction distinguishes driver/passenger roles, screen geography, physical reactions and dialogue timing. This is an attributed production experiment in a brief clip, not an independently proven language rule or a new feature-length release.
