Two older films join the collection with their complete original releases: [**Witchcraft**](/films/witchcraft/), a 3:18 dark fantasy comedy about an inflated bounty, and [**Crop Confidential**](/films/crop-confidential/), a 5:33 surveillance thriller about moving evidence through a compromised system. Daniił Vołkaŭ shared both again on October 10; their YouTube descriptions establish September release dates. The longer films, rather than the short X excerpts, are the main watch links.

## Storyboarding as a planning tool

[Henry Daubrez](/artists/henry-daubrez/) has now explained the storyboarding automation he mentioned yesterday. He uses Claude Code and Opus to build a Three.js previs tool, then renders individual scenes into storyboard frames. His demonstration names Opus 5.5. This puts explicit shot planning before final imagery, without proving which released film used the system or how much time it saves.

## Why a long take can be expensive to finish

The new [Backrooms Nemoris process section](/films/backrooms-nemoris/) draws on Javi Lopez’s direct October 8 interview. He describes manual prompts, layered DaVinci Resolve sound and the difficulty of getting a long handheld passage to work continuously. Shorter coverage can be assembled from usable portions of different generations; a continuous take gives the editor less room to rescue material. His advice is grounded in this production, not a rule that every filmmaker must avoid handheld work.

## A festival shortlist to watch

Higgsfield [announced its Global Film Festival shortlist](https://x.com/higgsfield/status/2109002027955368258) on October 10, reporting **126 films selected from 67,223 entries**. The [official shortlist page](https://higgsfield.ai/ja/contests/higgsfield-global-film-festival) is live. Audience Choice voting closes **October 15 at 23:59 UTC**, according to the organiser. These are organiser-reported counts and an open voting period, not final awards or evidence that every shortlisted film belongs in this journal.
