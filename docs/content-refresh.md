## Editorial standard

Select filmmakers with a sustained body of work, complete narrative shorts, or clear formal authorship. Prefer identifiable dramatic intent, visual language, performance judgment, editing and continuity over popularity, resolution or model novelty. There is no arbitrary ten-minute minimum: a substantive shorter narrative can qualify. A 15-second test, trailer, tutorial or vendor demo is not a completed narrative film. Commercial support must be disclosed when relevant. A complete narrative commissioned, funded or produced by an AI tool maker (as Kling, Dreamina or Magnific do) is not branded content: include it on its merits and disclose the support. Announced features belong in a watchlist until evidence justifies a collection entry.

Music videos are collected as their own format (`format: "Music video"`), not as narrative shorts. Include an officially released video when it has an identifiable director or credited studio and clear directorial intent: a narrative, recurring characters or a sustained visual concept cut to the song. Exclude lyric videos, visualizer loops, model-launch or sponsored capability demos and clips without a released song. Set `genre: "Music video"`; do not add music genres. Credit the song artist in `musician` exactly as the release or creator credits them; do not infer a human performer from a project identity. The production notebook, sourcing and image rules are the same as for films.

Series list their episodes in frontmatter (`episodes`, in release order); a series without episodes fails the build. Each inline episode needs a status and a `source` that evidences that episode, plus a number (as the maker numbers it) or a title; use `episodeNoun: "Chapter"` or `"Part"` when the maker does. Add `addedAt` with the refresh date when an episode joins an existing series: that is what marks the card "New episode", and moves the series up its rail. Include a continuing episode in a journal note when there is a substantive story, craft or production development; do not substitute addedAt for the authored editorial assessment. Use `status: "Coming soon"` for an announced episode; it is listed but never playable. The series `status` must equal its most available episode's, and its `watchUrl` must be the first playable episode's link (where new viewers start), not the latest; the build checks both. An anthology story with its own entry is linked with `- film: "<slug>"` instead of being described twice. Never list an episode the sources do not establish, and do not move a series runtime onto an episode without knowing which episode it describes.

Every film and artist needs an original explanation of why it matters. Describe the actual filmmaking problem and what the work demonstrates or tests. Preserve useful public analysis from the original watch, but exclude personal plans, private product strategy, and copied transcripts. Do not claim to have watched or critically reviewed a complete film unless you actually did. A festival selection or a production milestone is evidence of that fact, not proof of artistic quality.

## Writing for readers

Readers come to Latent Matinee to discover AI films worth watching and to learn how they were made. Write for them, not for an auditor.

- **Lead with the work.** Say what it is and the concrete choice that makes it worth a viewer's time (staging, editing, performance, sound, visual form, structure). Avoid plot summary as a substitute for a reason.
- **Show how it was made.** Tools and models with versions, the workflow and the order of steps, what was generated and what was made by hand, team size and roles, time, cost or sponsorship, and the specific problems the makers solved (character consistency, lip sync, continuity, voice, editing to a song). Attribute every claim to whoever disclosed it, with a source. Behind-the-scenes videos, making-of threads, interviews and full release descriptions are the best sources.
- **Keep the verification log private.** Public copy does not discuss runtime discrepancies between players, "sampled playback", what was or was not converted into a budget, or which checks were run. No "What was verified" sections. Record those details in the private run summary.
- **Stay honest without hedging every line.** Never claim to have watched a complete film or describe what the evidence cannot support. When an important fact is unknown, say so once and plainly ("The tools have not been disclosed."), and only when a reader would want to know.
- Short paragraphs, concrete nouns, no hype adjectives, no marketing copy. Paraphrase sources; never copy descriptions or transcripts.

## Watch links

Prefer the highest-quality playable source, because the site embeds YouTube full-screen:

1. The creator's or credited studio's own YouTube upload of the full work.
2. An official publisher's YouTube upload (festival, distributor, curator, or the platform that commissioned it).
3. Vimeo or another embeddable official upload.
4. An official platform project page or screening room (Higgsfield, CLOWD/Rad, festival players).
5. An X post, only when nothing above exists.

Verify a YouTube link with `scripts/youtube.mjs video` before using it (see the runbook): public or unlisted, embeddable, the full work rather than a trailer, teaser, excerpt or Short, and the right uploader. When a better source is found for an existing record, switch `watchUrl` (and the episode `watchUrl`) and keep the earlier link in `sources`. A link that `youtube.mjs audit` reports as deleted or private loses its Watch now status until a working official release is found; an embedding-disabled link stays playable but should be replaced when an embeddable official upload exists.

## Homepage features

The daily editorial refresh owns the ordered `content/featured.json` slate. Make a provisional selection after reading intake, then review it again before the publish or no-change decision so works found during the run can qualify. Compare the current picks with recent additions and substantive updates since the last slate change. Replace an incumbent when a new candidate makes the lineup stronger. If keeping the entire slate, record which candidates were considered and why the current lineup serves readers better in the private run summary. Feature exactly five works with credited artwork, at most one work per creator and at most two music videos. Use the film slug, the original Madrid calendar date it entered the slate as `since`, and a short editorial `reason`. Cite original audience or award evidence in the reason when it affects the pick. Weigh audience interest alongside recent releases, new episodes, availability, range across formats, and whether the actual image reads well at desktop and mobile hero crops. Prefer a representative still over an interface frame or an illegible poster crop. Keep popularity in context across platforms and release ages.

Because the carousel plays YouTube full-screen and readers want to learn how films are made, prefer picks with an embeddable official YouTube full release and a substantive production account on the film page. A pick without an embeddable player, or without production notes, needs a specific reason recorded privately. Write each `reason` as one or two sentences that tell a browsing reader why to watch and what is interesting about how it was made; a plot synopsis alone is not a reason.

Retain a worthwhile pick with its original date for at most seven days. Seven days is a ceiling. Rotate earlier when a stronger candidate arrives. Check recent slate history before bringing back a pick, and give it at least one week off after removal. Change the order only for a reason. Verify that the rendered home has the intended lineup, no repeated creator, loaded artwork and working carousel controls before committing and again on the public site. An unchanged slate does not require a commit.

### Opening-slot rotation

Slot one is the work visible on a fresh homepage load. Review it separately from slate membership. Give the strongest newly selected work first consideration for the lead. Keep an incumbent there only when a specific editorial reason supports it, and record that reason privately. A film may open the homepage for at most two consecutive Madrid dates. Use the date of its latest promotion from the Git history of `content/featured.json`, or the date it joined the slate if it debuted first. When its lead turn ends, promote the strongest eligible pick already on the slate or select a stronger new work. A move between slots preserves the original `since` date. Keep the existing seven-day slate limit and one-week cooldown after removal.

Before committing and after deployment, verify that a fresh homepage load shows the intended opening work without selecting a slide. Then verify the carousel controls and the opening artwork at desktop and mobile sizes.

### Editorial judgment

Compare the strongest candidates with the current lineup and with related works in the catalogue and recent feature history. For a finalist, use the finished cut or substantial sequence-level evidence from the release or making-of. Look for a concrete choice in staging, editing, performance, sound or visual form that holds across the work and serves its idea. Record what you checked and any limit on viewing. A new song, outfit, setting, high view count or handsome thumbnail alone is insufficient. For returning characters or formats, identify what the new work does differently and what that adds for the viewer.

Assess the lineup as a whole. Feature at most one work built around the same recurring persona or closely related creative formula, even across different creator credits. For example, a theatrical storybook musical can warrant the Claudia slot because the book, full-size sets and silhouette sequence shape the song. A further Claudia pop release needs a material formal or dramatic advance to earn that slot. Consider stronger alternatives across forms, including older work that still merits discovery. Keep all five slots filled. Add a newly selected work at the front, shift retained works right and remove only the displaced fifth work; preserve the retained works’ original dates. Replace an expired or unsuitable work one for one. Do not shrink the slate or reshuffle unaffected works without a specific editorial reason. In the private run summary, record a concrete reason for each change and explain why the strongest near-miss lost its place. Keep public slate reasons focused on the selected work.

### PostHog feedback

At each homepage review, run `node --env-file-if-exists=.env scripts/featured-engagement.mjs` from the canonical checkout. The read-only EU PostHog query returns 28 days of Latent Matinee carousel counts grouped by work, feature start date, visible slot, format and watch mode. Configure the numeric `POSTHOG_PROJECT_ID` and a project-restricted `POSTHOG_QUERY_API_KEY` with `query:read` in the local environment or git-ignored `.env`. Keep credentials and engagement counts out of Git, public copy and slate reasons. If the query fails or is empty, continue with editorial evidence and avoid inferring low interest.

Use watch clicks per impression as the main audience signal where there is a direct watch action. Compare against works in similar slots and watch modes, with format as context. Use details clicks per impression for works without a direct watch action. Tab selections measure active curiosity and can overlap the other actions. The site counts each action once per work per page visit for consenting visitors, so these are visit-based signals. An impression means the active slide was at least half visible for one second or the visitor actively chose or clicked it. Watch clicks measure intent, including outbound links. A details click followed by a watch action elsewhere is outside this report.

Treat fewer than 50 impressions in a comparable group as directional evidence only. Sparse or unavailable analytics never delay an editorial rotation. Leave room for new and underexposed work. Across multiple rotations, look for repeatable patterns in artwork, formats and availability, and use them to inform future picks alongside editorial judgment. Keep all diversity, rotation and artwork rules.

## Festivals and competitions

`content/festivals/` holds one Markdown file per AI film festival or competition, with its editions listed oldest first. Results live only there: artist pages ("Awards & selections"), film pages ("Festival recognition"), and the Festivals index all read them. Never copy an award into a second structured place.

### Listing bar

List only properly organised festivals and competitions that publish their results:

- a named organiser, a public call for entries or rules, and a defined jury, judging or selection process;
- officially published results from the organiser for at least one completed edition.

An AI strand of an established festival qualifies on its parent's standing (`kind: "Strand of an established festival"`, `parent`). An inaugural edition qualifies only when an established institution runs it; otherwise keep it as a private lead until it publishes official results. Exclude pay-to-enter laurel mills, monthly award mills without a programme, jury and results, hashtag campaigns and model-launch challenges without published judging. Record exclusions and their reason in research-watchlist.md. Always disclose platform or brand organisers and sponsors (`kind: "Brand or platform"`, `sponsors`; `"Foundation prize"` for a foundation-run prize such as XPRIZE; otherwise `"Independent"`). `type` is `Festival` for events built around a programme or screening, `Competition` for calls judged online.

### Records

- Reuse the slug of the festival's name, without a year. Name editions as the organiser does ("2026", "Season 2").
- `milestones` contain dates the organiser published, each with a direct `source`. For a past event the organiser's pages no longer date, a reputable press report may date it; say so in the milestone `note`. Upcoming deadlines and dates always come from the organiser. Never estimate a date; leave an unannounced step out and say so in the milestone note or prose (an open call without a dated deadline shows as "Deadline not yet announced"). Change a date only after checking the organiser's current page, and note the change under a dated heading.
- Never write a status. Entries open, Closing soon, Public voting, Happening now, Upcoming, Results pending and Results are worked out from the milestones, at build time and again in the reader's browser.
- `submitUrl` points only to the organiser's own entry page or its official FilmFreeway listing, and needs a sourced deadline.
- Brief, eligibility, prizes, jury and entry or shortlist counts use the organiser's wording and are attributed. A required tool is a contest rule, not evidence of what a particular film used.
- `awards` record only results the organiser published. A maker-only claim stays attributed in the film's text. Use `credit` exactly as the organiser writes it. Link `film` when the work is in the catalogue; otherwise give its `title`. Use `artist` for an award to a person or a co-credited maker. Decisive results (Winner, Runner-up, Jury selection, Audience choice, Honourable mention) need a dated `Results`, `Ceremony` or `Screening` milestone no later than `lastVerifiedAt`; prefer the organiser's `Results` date. With a catalogue `film`, `title` may name the awarded episode or cut. Map the organiser's own ranks (Gold, Silver, Merit) onto these results and keep the organiser's label in `category`. A shortlist, finalist list, nomination or official selection is not a win.
- When results join an existing record, set `addedAt` to the run date on each new award.
- Treat published awards as a film and filmmaker discovery queue. Traverse every edition, including historical results, and reconcile every award row against canonical films and artists. Research every unlinked work and any linked work still missing an official full release or credited artwork. Reuse verified aliases and slugs. Add missing completed narrative and eligible music-video winners, runners-up, audience winners and jury mentions and their credited filmmakers or studios in the same run. A confirmed festival screening of a completed work qualifies even if the full film is unavailable online. Use `Festival screening` when that is the strongest evidence and leave playback empty. Keep trailers, pitches, ads and model demos outside the film catalogue unless they independently qualify under the editorial standard. Record a sourced disposition for each unresolved or excluded work.
- Apply the daily discovery workflow in “Research and publishing” to each award: start with official results and programme details, then check original filmmaker or studio profiles, public posts and credits, linked making-of material, festival and distributor players, and official video channels. Search alternate titles, original-language titles and credit spellings. Prioritise official full-film uploads and verify the current player, uploader, title and runtime. A trailer, excerpt or pitch cannot be the full-film watch link. Check programme stills and creator artwork independently of playback and credit the image source.
- Do all YouTube searches, channel checks and release verification with `scripts/youtube.mjs` (API key only). A signed-out in-app browser visit is an occasional fallback for what the API cannot show, within the runbook's limits; never use the user's own Chrome.
- Festival artwork needs alt text, credit and source, as film artwork does; never use a logo as artwork. Without it, the card uses the newest winning film's credited artwork automatically.
