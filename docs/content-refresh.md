## Editorial standard

Select filmmakers with a sustained body of work, complete narrative shorts, or clear formal authorship. Prefer identifiable dramatic intent, visual language, performance judgment, editing and continuity over popularity, resolution or model novelty. There is no arbitrary ten-minute minimum: a substantive shorter narrative can qualify. A 15-second test, trailer, tutorial or vendor demo is not a completed narrative film. Commercial support must be disclosed when relevant. Announced features belong in a watchlist until evidence justifies a collection entry.

Music videos are collected as their own format (`format: "Music video"`), not as narrative shorts. Include an officially released video when it has an identifiable director or credited studio and clear directorial intent: a narrative, recurring characters or a sustained visual concept cut to the song. Exclude lyric videos, visualizer loops, model-launch or sponsored capability demos and clips without a released song. Set `genre: "Music video"`; do not add music genres. Credit the song artist in `musician` exactly as the release or creator credits them; do not infer a human performer from a project identity. The production notebook, sourcing and image rules are the same as for films.

Series list their episodes in frontmatter (`episodes`, in release order); a series without episodes fails the build. Each inline episode needs a status and a `source` that evidences that episode, plus a number (as the maker numbers it) or a title; use `episodeNoun: "Chapter"` or `"Part"` when the maker does. Add `addedAt` with the refresh date when an episode joins an existing series: that is what marks the card "New episode", moves the series up its rail and adds a What's New item, so a journal note is only needed for something substantive beyond the release. Use `status: "Coming soon"` for an announced episode; it is listed but never playable. The series `status` must equal its most available episode's, and its `watchUrl` must be the first playable episode's link (where new viewers start), not the latest; the build checks both. An anthology story with its own entry is linked with `- film: "<slug>"` instead of being described twice. Never list an episode the sources do not establish, and do not move a series runtime onto an episode without knowing which episode it describes.

Every film and artist needs an original explanation of why it matters. Describe the actual filmmaking problem and what the work demonstrates or tests. Preserve useful public analysis from the original watch, but exclude personal plans, private product strategy, and copied transcripts. Do not claim to have watched or critically reviewed a complete film unless you actually did. A festival selection or a production milestone is evidence of that fact, not proof of artistic quality.

## Inclusion with incomplete disclosures

A verified narrative release and identifiable creator or credited studio are enough to consider inclusion on editorial merit. Missing individual credits, budget, exact models or team size do not alone justify deferral. Publish supported facts, label unknowns, and keep researching. Check original full-release descriptions, creator portfolios and linked production sources before deferring a lead. Never imply full viewing or invent credits. Recent additions automatically appear in What’s New; add a dated journal note for substantive changes to existing entries, not routine verification.
